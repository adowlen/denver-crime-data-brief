## LinkedIn company page post

48.3% in 2022. 24.8% in 2026.

That is the share of Denver's reported offenses involving a motor vehicle, in matched Jan 1-Oct 13 windows. Vehicle reports fell by half between 2022 and 2025 (28,219 to 13,923). Reports not involving a vehicle are at their highest level of the six windows measured (36,362 in the 2026 window, up from 28,391 in 2021), which is why the citywide total ticked up 1.6% this year, the first increase since the 2022 peak.

The fall in the reported numbers is one story: cars. Today's brief decomposes all 383,643 DPD offense records by category and neighborhood, answers the traffic question (the offenses file contains zero traffic records; accidents are a separate file of about 18,000 reports a year), and prints what the file cannot show, including that no sexual-assault offense type appears in the public file.

Full brief with methods and data: https://github.com/adowlen/denver-crime-data-brief

## X thread

1/ Denver crime reports peaked in 2022 and fell 18.7% by 2025 (matched Jan 1-Oct 13 windows). The entire net decline is 4 vehicle offense types, down 14,296 reports. Everything else rose by 3,389.

2/ Vehicle share of the ledger: 48.3% (2022), 29.3% (2025), 24.8% (2026 window). Auto theft reports: 11,946, then 4,518, then 3,394.

3/ Reports not involving a vehicle are at their highest of the six windows: 28,391 in 2021, 36,362 in 2026. That is why the 2026 window total is up 1.6%, the first increase since the 2022 peak.

4/ The traffic question, answered from the flags: DPD's offenses file marks IS_TRAFFIC on 0 of 383,643 records. Traffic accidents are a separate published file, about 18,000 reports a year.

5/ Neighborhoods: Five Points has the most reports (3,627 in the 2025 window) and rose 4.7% while the city fell. The steep drops are car-oriented areas (Hampden South -45.2%). Civic Center's ledger is only 8.6% vehicle reports, and it rose 35.1%.

6/ Printed caveats: these are reports, not incidence. No sexual-assault offense type appears in the public file. Drug lines track enforcement practice. Full brief, methods, and data: https://github.com/adowlen/denver-crime-data-brief
